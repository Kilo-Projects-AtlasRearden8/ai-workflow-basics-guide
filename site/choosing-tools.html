Choosing AI Tools

Chapter 1 of the AI Workflow Basics Guide — plain English, step by step.

There are a lot of AI tools out there, and more show up every week. It is easy to feel like you are already behind. You are not. Most people only need one or two tools to get started, and the best tool is the one you will actually use.

This chapter walks you through choosing your first AI tools without spending money you do not need to spend and without handing over private data.

Step 1: Decide what you want to do

Before you look at any tool, write down one real task you want help with. Examples:

  • Writing a book blurb or newsletter
  • Summarizing meeting notes
  • Turning a rough idea into a blog post
  • Organizing paperwork or a simple budget
  • Writing a bit of code for a personal project

Pick one task. You do not need a strategy. You need a starting point.

Start with an AI chatbot. Everything else builds on that habit.

Step 3: Choose between cloud tools and local tools

Cloud tools (websites and apps)

Examples: the free tier of a mainstream AI assistant, browser-based chat tools.

Pros: No setup, works on any device, often free to start.
Cons: Your text goes to someone else's server, you need an internet connection, free tiers usually have limits.

Local tools (run on your computer)

Examples: a local LLM runner such as Ollama or LM Studio, running a small model on your laptop.

Pros: Your data stays on your machine, works offline, no account needed, no usage limits.
Cons: You need to install software first, your computer needs to be reasonably modern, models take up disk space.

Beginner recommendation: Start with a free cloud chatbot to learn how to talk to AI. When you want privacy or offline access, try a local tool next.

Step 4: Check these five things before you commit

Ask about any tool you are considering:

  1. Cost: Is there a free tier? What happens when you run out of free uses?
  2. Privacy: Can you read the privacy policy in plain language? Does your input train someone else's model?
  3. Ease of use: Can you start within ten minutes of installing?
  4. Portability: Can you copy your conversations out? (Look for export or copy/paste.)
  5. Offline: Does it work without internet? (Only local tools do.)

If a tool fails more than two of these, keep looking.

Step 6: Your first session (10 minutes)

  1. Open your chosen tool.
  2. Type this: "I am new to AI. I want help with [your one task from Step 1]. Ask me three questions so you can give me a good first draft."
  3. Answer the questions.
  4. Read the result. Change anything you do not like.
  5. Save what you liked somewhere you can find it again.

That is it. You have used an AI tool.

A quick word on cost. Free tiers change often. This guide does not list prices because they will be wrong by the time you read this. Check the tool's pricing page before you enter payment details. You can do almost everything in this guide with free tools.
